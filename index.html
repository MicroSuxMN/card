⭐ 4× Membership Rewards detailsAmex terms
How it works: American Express awards 4× Membership Rewards on the two eligible categories with the most posted spend in each billing period. The winning categories are calculated automatically and can change from one billing period to the next.
Earning rate4 points per $1: 1 base point + 3 additional points.
SelectionTop 2 of 6 eligible categories, determined from posted Card Account transactions each billing period.
Annual capFirst $150,000 in combined eligible purchases per calendar year across the Card Account.
After the capEligible category purchases above $150,000 earn 1×.
Employee CardsEligible purchases on Basic and Employee Cards are included across the same Card Account.
At the capUp to 600,000 Membership Rewards points on $150,000 of qualifying 4× spend.
Tie ruleOnly two categories receive the bonus even when more than two categories have equal eligible spend.
Other travel3× on eligible flights and prepaid hotels booked through AmexTravel.com or the Amex Travel App.
Everything else1× on other eligible purchases and on the four non-winning 4× categories for that billing period.

📣U.S. advertising in select media

QualifiesOnline, television and radio advertising purchased directly from U.S. media providers to promote a business. Amex examples include Facebook, Yahoo, NBC and CBS Radio.
Watch forDirect-mail services, list or lead generation, and charges that are not coded as eligible advertising.

💻U.S. electronic goods, software & cloud systems

QualifiesDirect U.S. purchases from electronic-goods retailers or software/cloud-system providers. Amex examples include Dell, Newegg, Adobe and Salesforce.
Watch forBroad marketplaces such as Amazon or eBay; Target or Walmart; wireless carriers; app-store or aggregator purchases; consulting/management services; third-party resellers or payment accounts.

🍽️U.S. restaurants

QualifiesRestaurants in the U.S., including takeout and delivery. Fast-food restaurants can qualify when the transaction is coded as a restaurant.
Watch forBars, nightclubs, convenience stores, supermarkets, grocery stores, cafeterias, caterers, theatre clubs, breweries, bakeries and meeting venues. Restaurants inside hotels, casinos, event venues, food courts or campuses may code as the host merchant.

⛽U.S. gas stations

QualifiesGasoline at U.S. merchants whose primary business is selling gasoline. A qualifying gas station may also operate a convenience store.
Watch forFuel sold by superstores, supermarkets or warehouse clubs; marina fuel; and commercial fuel such as jet fuel.

🚇Transit

QualifiesMerchants whose primary business is transit, including trains, taxis, rideshares, ferries, tolls, parking, buses and subways.
Watch forAirfare, car rentals, cruises and trams are not treated as transit.

📱U.S. wireless telephone service

QualifiesMonthly wireless service charges paid directly to a U.S. wireless provider. Amex examples include AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon.
Watch forPhones, accessories or repairs; authorized retailers or resellers; bundled TV, internet or VoIP; VoIP service; Google Fi; and third-party payment accounts.
Merchant coding controls the result. Amex uses posted transaction data and merchant category codes. A charge may miss 4× when a third party sells, processes or submits it; when a third-party payment account is used; or when Amex cannot identify the purchase as eligible. Eligible purchases exclude fees, interest, travelers checks, prepaid-card reloads and cash equivalents; returns and credits reduce eligible spend.
